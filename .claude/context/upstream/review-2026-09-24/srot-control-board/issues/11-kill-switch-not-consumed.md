# The thruster kill switch is display-only: while armed with thruster power cut, the controllers wind up and AUTO moves still "complete"

Part of the 2026-09-24 ecosystem review (tracking issue in fh1m/mongla_ws, link to follow).

## Summary
The second board reports the thruster kill switch over ESP-NOW. The control loop copies it into `in.kill` and never reads it. With the kill switch pulled while armed:
- The attitude and depth controllers keep integrating against a vehicle that cannot respond.
- `SROT_MOVE` legs run their timed profile and report success.
- When power is restored the accumulated demand is applied all at once.

## Evidence
At commit `f1d3ba9`.

`src/tasks/task_control_loop.cpp:41` and `:105`: the value is stored and has no further readers.
```cpp
    bool       kill;
    ...
            in.depth = s.depth_m; in.kill = s.kill_switch;
```
`grep -rn "in.kill" src` finds only line 105. The other readers of `kill_switch` are display and telemetry only: `task_ui_status.cpp:80`, `task_lora_sd.cpp:99`, and `mav_stream.cpp:65` / `:846` (`NAMED_VALUE_FLOAT KILL`).

`src/drivers/espnow_link.cpp:49` states the intent directly:
```cpp
    kill = f ? s_kill : false;   // link lost → don't assert kill (display-only)
```

**Correction to the audit that raised this:** the claim that "thrust_trim learns against 0 RPM" does **not** hold. `thrust_trim::update()` skips thrusters with no telemetry (`thrust_trim.cpp:65`), and unpowered ESCs drop out of `esc_present` after 500 ms. `THR_TRIM_EN` also defaults to 0. The integrator and move effects below do hold.

## Failure scenario
1. On the pool deck the vehicle is armed in `DEPTH_HOLD` or `STABILIZE`. A handler pulls the thruster kill so they can reposition it, which is the purpose of the switch.
2. The vehicle is tilted and lifted. Roll, pitch and depth errors persist, so the rate and depth integrators wind to their limits. `SROT_MOVE forward 3 s` completes with `ACCEPTED` at 100 % while nothing moved (compare #8).
3. The kill is reinserted. The thrusters come back to a saturated I-term and a stale yaw hold, and the vehicle lurches in someone's hands.
4. The same applies to the in-water "kill to recover" procedure.

## Suggested fix
Treat `in.kill && in.armed` as "outputs inhibited":
- Hold the PID integrators, or `reset()` them, and freeze `feedforward` trim learning.
- Re-capture the yaw hold and depth target on the edge where the kill is released.
- Pause any AUTO move and report it as `MAV_RESULT_TEMPORARILY_REJECTED`, or `FAILED` with a reason. It must not report success.
- `STATUSTEXT "thruster power cut"` on the edge, and `"thruster power restored - controllers reset"` on the release.
- Optionally, refuse to arm while the kill is asserted. #12 notes that KILL=0 is ambiguous, so fix its semantics first.

The ESP-NOW link has no authentication (a separate issue in this batch). Do not let kill force a **disarm**. Inhibiting integration is safe even if the value is spoofed.

## How to verify
- **Bench (props off, second board attached):** arm in STABILIZE, tilt the board 20° by hand, and pull the kill for 5 s. Watch the rate I-terms, which need a debug NVF such as `IR`/`IP`/`ID`. Today they rail. After the fix they are held or reset. Release the kill with the board level: the demand must start from about 0, with no step.
- Run `SROT_MOVE` with the kill pulled. It must not report `ACCEPTED` at 100 %.
- **Injection test:** delete the `in.kill` branch and confirm the I-term check fails.

## Severity: Medium
The lurch on restore happens when a person has hands on the vehicle, and moves report success with the power cut.
